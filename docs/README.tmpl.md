# RNNTranslator

RNNTranslator is an English-to-Japanese translator written as a circuit, not trained: a hand-written rule transducer whose parser and printer are compiled, module by module, into two cells of sparse ReLU matrices — an encoder that reads one token at a time and a decoder that prints one morpheme at a time. Hypothesis: a language is a small circuit of tables and bounded control, and such a circuit can be written down, verified exhaustively, and compiled into weights whose every nonzero has a name. It is evaluated on the Tanaka-corpus dev ({{f:rules.dev.n}} sentences) and test ({{f:rules.test.n}} sentences) splits of small_parallel_enja, pinned by commit and hash.

## Architecture

![Architecture](docs/architecture.svg)

The lexical layer merges multiword tokens and gives each token its readings from the {{f:tables.count}} tables, and the semantic layer generates the Japanese from the frame. The parser layer has one interface and two implementations, `RuleParser` and `MatrixParser`, and the matrix implementation is meant to agree with the rule implementation wherever the matrix can represent the parse. `RuleParser` is a bounded transducer: a {{f:parser.window}}-token window, a frame stack of depth at most {{f:parser.max_depth}}, at most {{f:parser.width}} live readings resolved within {{f:parser.hold}} tokens, and no rewinds. The rule parser refuses an object-form or reflexive pronoun as a subject and a finite verb that disagrees in number with a pronoun or plural subject. `MatrixParser` runs the same machine as two recurrent cells of {{f:cell.layers}} sparse ReLU layers per step — an encoder that reads the sentence into frames and a decoder that prints from them — with the noun-phrase module, the PP machine, the predicate, the copula and the printing rules translated by hand and the rest of the clause-level control extracted from traces of the rule parser. Two feedback edges cross the layers: the noun's use-verb rewrites the verb (she took a rest → 彼女は休んだ), and the parser's state decides which reading of a token is used (i rest → 休む, i need a rest → 休息). The second edge is the lexical layer's part inside the encoder: the table rows that join the frame's verb with the window — the idiom tails, the particle after a verb, the use nouns — are AND units over the verb block and the window's table keys, ORed into the hit predicates the rules read, so the runner hands the encoder the window alone.

## Encoder, context, decoder

![The two cells and the context](docs/cell.svg)

![The two cells, unit by unit](docs/cell_neurons.svg)

The compiled machine is two recurrent cells with a context between them: the encoder's state is a register file of one-hot blocks (the control point, the slots of the working frame, the pending preposition, and the shift and flush units), its input is one-hot over the readings of the window, and one step is {{f:cell.layers}} ReLU layers followed by a readout of zeros and ones into the next state; the decoder is a second cell of the same shape, whose state is the printing position, the sentence block, the emit, down and up units and the output morpheme, and whose input is one frame, unit for unit the encoder's slot block. In both cells every weight is plus or minus one, except a large negative weight that silences a rule whose literal the cell cannot see, and every bias is an integer threshold. The second picture is the two cells unit by unit, in the textbook style: circles are units, columns are the layers, arrows are the weights; in the encoder one write into the object block is traced in colour, in the decoder its mirror — a printing position, the emit unit, one slot read and one form lookup into the output — in orange, and between them the context, with the attention head on one of its frames. A slot write is a gated copy: the rules that write a block are ORed into a block gate and, per source, a source gate; the next layer holds one write unit per value, which fires when the source gate and that source value are both on, and one keep unit per value, which the block gate cuts; and the readout sums keep and write back into the block, so one gate fans a rule out over the values of a block as a demultiplexer and clears the old value in the same step. The decoder shares nothing with the encoder but the context: no unit of the encoder emits and no unit of the decoder reads a token; a reading rule consumes a token and writes slots, and a printing rule reads the frame under the attention, the sentence block and the decoder's own last output, emits a morpheme or moves the attention, and writes no slot. The context is one frame per clause, in the order the clauses closed, and the sentence block of the encoder's final state: when the flush unit is on, the slot block of the encoder's state is appended to the context — at a clause boundary, where the frame is first marked with what it is to the frame beside it and the slots are then cleared, each by the gate that writes it, for the next clause to be read into, and at the end of the sentence. The decoder reads the context by hard attention, one frame at a time: the attention head starts on the first frame and moves to the next or the previous one when the down or the up unit is on, so a that-clause is printed where Japanese wants it — the outer clause's subject and arguments, then down for the clause and と, then back up for the outer predicate — and a that-clause inside a that-clause is one more frame under the same rules. A question mark read after a that-clause, when the outer frame has already left the encoder, reaches the decoder in the sentence block. A subordinate clause — when, if, because, although — is a frame beside its main clause's, in the order the two were read, and is printed first either way, which the decoder can do because nothing is printed before the encoder is done: it starts on the first frame, steps over a subordinate clause that was read first, prints the main clause's front adverb, moves the attention to the subordinate frame — up or down, as the main frame's mark says — prints it with が on its subject, plain forms and its conjunction's ending, and moves back for the rest of the main clause. The conjunction is read by the clause rules, written by hand: a clause read first opens on it and closes at its comma, where its frame is flushed and the main clause's frame is marked; a clause read second opens where the PP machine of the main clause returns at the conjunction, its second exit, and there the main frame is marked and flushed; and a question mark read after it reaches the decoder in the sentence block, as after a that-clause. What a conjunction changes in its clause — the past under if, once and after, the negative under unless, the potential under so that — is written into the frame by the rule that flushes it, so the frame the decoder reads is the clause the rule generator prints, and every conjunction of the table has its ending after the clause's finite form; unless slices that form in the rule generator, and the printing rules refuse it. The context holds one subordinate clause beside a main clause: a second one, a that-clause inside one, and one that opens when its main clause has already begun are refused by the encoder, and a conjunction that also reads as a preposition (before, after, since) is read as the preposition after a main clause, and while as a coordinator, the rule parser's first readings. Hypothesis: every other inner clause the rule parser folds into a string — a coordinated clause, a relative clause, an infinitive — is a frame with a mark of its own kind, read at a boundary and printed by a move of the attention in the same way. Every mechanism of the encoder has a mirror in the decoder — the shift unit that advances the read head mirrors an emit unit that advances the print head, the flush unit that appends a frame to the context mirrors the down and up units that move the attention over it, a write gate that fans a rule out over the values of a block mirrors a read gate that selects a block of the frame onto the output, the USE lookup that rewrites the verb from its noun mirrors a form lookup that selects the verb's stem form, the suffix following as a constant the flags choose, and a copy with no source mirrors a read with nothing in the block or a form the verb has not, both of which refuse. The output block holds one morpheme, a one-hot over slot values, verb and adjective stem forms, particles, suffixes and punctuation: the readout sets it from the read and lookup units on a step that prints and keeps it on a step that does not, and an output lexical layer maps each unit to its string the way the input lexical layer maps each string to its readings. The decoder is autoregressive: the output block stays in its state and its rules read it — a stem is printed with its conjugation class, a class predicate is one unit, the OR of the block's units of that class, and the finishing stage of the predicate takes the class from there instead of from its position, so it has one position per mode for every predicate whose stem is out and none per class. A frame's clause kind decides the form its predicate ends in: a clause marked te or stem, spliced into another like a that-clause, prints up to the te form or the masu stem of whatever predicate its registers build — a lookup for a plain verb, the ending of the class of the stem just printed otherwise, never a slice of a string — and hands the attention back with nothing after it; the rule generator prints such a clause the same way, both refuse a predicate that has no such form, and a bare -ing clause under while (while walking, 歩きながら) is the first construction read by the encoder that ends this way. Hypothesis: the constructions the rule generator still builds by slicing a string — the purpose and manner clauses, an adjective's infinitive, the te link of coordination, the causative — print through these two modes with a guard each, and the tense after a causative stem is chosen from the output block once the attention is back on the outer frame. The printing rules are written in the normal form of the noun-phrase module — per position, an ordered decision over slot predicates whose terminals emit one token or move the attention: a constant morpheme, a slot's value, or a form of the verb or the adjective from the morphology table — and the `printer` action checks them against `generate()` on every sentence the rule parser parses and on a sweep of {{f:printer.sweep.frames|,}} frames over the predicates they test, a that-clause under a reporting verb and around a fixed inner clause among them, a subordinate clause under every conjunction on either side of a main clause and a clause that ends at its te form or its masu stem, so the printer is one more chain of the lattice, agreeing with, refusing or misfiring against the rule generator on every sentence. The compiled machine runs unchanged under PyTorch, each cell a module whose {{f:cell.layers}} layers are sparse matrices held as fixed buffers: the `torch` action steps the scipy runner and the torch runner side by side on every dev and test sentence, the encoder and then the decoder, and their state vectors agree in single precision at every one of the {{f:torch.steps|,}} steps compared, the read head, the context, the attention head and the print head being the only operations outside the matrices.

## Normal form

A control point's rules have one canonical form, a reduced ordered decision diagram: tests follow a fixed order of the cell's variables — the window first, then the state — identical sub-diagrams are shared, a test whose branches agree is removed, and the result is the same object whatever order the rules came in, so two rule sets that define the same function are equal as diagrams. A path of the diagram is a rule with no redundant literal, and it ends in a terminal (what to do), a gap (no rule covers that region) or a conflict (two rules with different outcomes cover it). The diagram has a text form, written and read by `canon.py`, that parses back to the same diagram. The diagram ranges over feasible inputs, not over literals treated as independent: a window slot's values are the lexicon's tokens, each as the set of predicates the lexical layer gives it, a state slot's values are what the rules can write into it or nothing, the PP slots fill in order, and a derived predicate, a count, a table hit or the USE pair is expanded into base literals before it is tested, so no path is a combination no input can be. Every control point of both cells is complete and consistent — every feasible input reaches exactly one outcome, a refusal included: the hand-written modules by their otherwise-rules, a table position refusing a value outside its table, the induced control points by the refusals the compiler materialises where a guard fails or the traces disagreed, and the return states by its closing refusal for a return address no caller serves. On the whole machine the `matrix` action builds the diagram of all {{f:canon.control_points|,}} control points ({{f:canon.nodes|,}} nodes) and finds {{f:canon.incomplete}} with a gap and {{f:canon.inconsistent}} with a conflict. Hypothesis: with every control point total, a hand-written module equals the rules induced for the same control point exactly when their diagrams are equal, so a hand translation of one of the paths still extracted is checked by comparing the two diagrams.

## Harness

Every commit runs the harness on the staged tree through the pre-commit hook, and the commit is refused if any item of the lattice moves down or an unjudged output changes without a verdict. Each action declares its inputs and runs in a sandbox that holds only those inputs, cached under `.cache/actions/` by the hash of the inputs, `uv.lock` and the interpreter. Every dependency is pinned with its sha256 in `uv.lock` ({{f:deps.count}} packages). The corpus is pinned to commit {{f:corpus.commit}} with a sha256 and line count per file, and is not vendored. Every table row, the corpus lock and the baseline are validated against JSON Schemas (draft {{f:schema.draft}}), with uniqueness and cross-table references declared in `schemas/tables/integrity.json`, and every sentence the baseline cites is verified against the pinned corpus line. The file tree above is compared with the tracked files. The architecture and cell diagrams are rendered from `docs/architecture.puml` and `docs/cell.puml` by PlantUML {{f:plantuml.version}}, pinned by the jar's sha256 and laid out by the jar's own engine, so that rendering needs no other tool, the neuron drawing by `harness/render_neurons.py` with nothing but Python, and each committed SVG must match the hash recorded at render time, show the labels of its source and, for the drawing, be exactly what the script emits. Every number in this README is rendered from a named fact, and every sentence is either pinned to a test or declared a hypothesis in the appendix below. Retired files leave the tree, and what is worth reading later goes into `archive/` as a compressed blob that no text search sees into. Every open item of the plan is in `TODO.yaml`: its context is statements pinned like this README's, it names an acceptance test in `harness/test_acceptance.py` that must fail while the item is open and pass once it is done, and every stub those tests are written against is exercised by them. `CLAUDE.md` states the rules every agent and human follows here, and every file and check it names exists.

The `rules` action measures coverage and char-BLEU of the transducer on dev and test and records every output. The `matrix` action compiles the encoder and the decoder on dev and test, runs them on every sentence — encoding, then decoding — and cross-tabulates the output against the rule transducer's. The `np` action checks `np_module` against `parse_np` over every window of at most {{f:np.max_len}} tokens, or {{f:np.full_len}} with `--full`. The `negatives` action runs the {{f:negatives.count}} ungrammatical sentences of `harness/negatives.json`, taken or adapted from the syntax literature, asserts that the rule parser refuses every one and accepts every grammatical control, and counts what the matrix does with them. The `torch` action compiles the same two cells, runs them under PyTorch as `cell_torch.py` builds them, and counts the sentences on which a torch cell's state ever differs from the scipy cell's. The `printer` action steps the printing rules of `gen_module.py` on the frame of every parsed sentence and on the sweep, and records what they print.

## The lattice

The state of the project is a product of small chains, one per item, and a change is an improvement exactly when no item moves down. A corpus sentence is WRONG, REFUSED, STIFF or RIGHT, where RIGHT is the reference or an output judged natural Japanese that says what the English says, STIFF is an output judged grammatical and faithful but not how it is said — better than a wrong output or a refusal, worse than a natural one — and an input adjudicated ungrammatical has refusal as its top; an output nobody has judged is UNKNOWN, below RIGHT but incomparable with the rest, so that a change away from it demands a verdict in `harness/adjudications.json`. A stiff or wrong verdict names its cause — an idiom, a sense, a preposition, a particle, the direction of a giving verb and so on — and the frontier groups the judged outputs by it. Hypothesis: the verdicts, which a model gave by reading each output against its English, hold to one standard — plain against polite is not judged unless the sentence's own words ask for one of them, in which case the other is wrong, and a giving verb that points the wrong way or a particle its verb does not take is wrong as a failure of agreement is. The matrix parser on a sentence is MISFIRE, GAP or AGREE against the rule parser, and so are the printing rules against the generator; a negative is ACCEPTED or REFUSED, a control is REFUSED or ACCEPTED, the exhaustive NP check and the printer's sweep are MISMATCHES or MATCH, and the torch run is DIVERGING or EXACT. Coverage and quality are views of this state, not gates. The frontier is the set of items below the top of their chain, grouped by what blocks them, and `uv run python harness/run.py frontier` prints it from the committed baseline.

Today the {{f:lattice.items|,}} items stand at: sentences RIGHT {{f:lattice.sentence.right}}, STIFF {{f:lattice.sentence.stiff}}, UNKNOWN {{f:lattice.sentence.unknown}}, REFUSED {{f:lattice.sentence.refused}}, WRONG {{f:lattice.sentence.wrong}}; matrix AGREE {{f:lattice.matrix.agree}}, GAP {{f:lattice.matrix.gap}}, MISFIRE {{f:lattice.matrix.misfire}}; negatives REFUSED {{f:lattice.negative.refused}} of {{f:negatives.count}} and controls ACCEPTED {{f:lattice.control.accepted}}; {{f:lattice.adjudications}} sentences adjudicated, {{f:quality.judges.model}} of the verdicts by a model. The frontier holds {{f:lattice.frontier.items|,}} items in {{f:lattice.frontier.groups}} groups.

## Where it stands

The rule transducer translates {{f:rules.test.covered}} of {{f:rules.test.n}} test sentences ({{f:rules.test.coverage_pct}}%) and {{f:rules.dev.covered}} of {{f:rules.dev.n}} dev sentences ({{f:rules.dev.coverage_pct}}%), with char-BLEU {{f:rules.test.bleu_covered|.1f}} and {{f:rules.dev.bleu_covered|.1f}} inside coverage. Of the {{f:quality.translated}} sentences it translates, the verdicts call {{f:quality.right}} right, {{f:quality.stiff}} stiff and {{f:quality.wrong}} wrong. The wrong outputs are blamed on a set phrase read word for word ({{f:quality.wrong.idiom}}), a word sense its argument rules out ({{f:quality.wrong.sense}}), a construction read as another ({{f:quality.wrong.structure}}), a preposition read apart from its noun ({{f:quality.wrong.preposition}}), a particle the verb does not take ({{f:quality.wrong.case}}), a form that is not Japanese ({{f:quality.wrong.morphology}}), a modal or a tense ({{f:quality.wrong.tense}}), a count or a quantifier ({{f:quality.wrong.quantity}}), a dummy it printed as それ ({{f:quality.wrong.it}}), a dropped word ({{f:quality.wrong.omission}}), a negation lost or widened ({{f:quality.wrong.negation}}), the direction of a giving verb ({{f:quality.wrong.giving}}) and a pronoun ({{f:quality.wrong.pronoun}}); the stiff ones on wording carried over from the English ({{f:quality.stiff.literal}}), a repeated pronoun ({{f:quality.stiff.pronoun}}), a sense ({{f:quality.stiff.sense}}), a preposition ({{f:quality.stiff.preposition}}), a quantity ({{f:quality.stiff.quantity}}), the order of the phrases ({{f:quality.stiff.order}}), an object left unsaid ({{f:quality.stiff.omission}}), a tense ({{f:quality.stiff.tense}}), a particle ({{f:quality.stiff.case}}) and an idiom ({{f:quality.stiff.idiom}}); and none on the register, since no sentence of the corpus asks for one. On the {{f:matrix.sentences}} dev and test sentences, the matrices translate {{f:matrix.identical}} exactly as the rule transducer does; they misfire silently on {{f:matrix.silent_misfires}} ({{f:matrix.wrong_frame}} different translations and {{f:matrix.matrix_parses_refused}} translations of sentences the rules refuse) and refuse {{f:matrix.refused_parseable}} sentences the rules translate. The encoder has {{f:encoder.rules|,}} rules ({{f:cell.rules.outer}} extracted, {{f:cell.rules.entry}} entry, {{f:cell.rules.exit}} exit, {{f:cell.rules.fail}} refusal, {{f:cell.rules.module}} from the noun-phrase module, {{f:cell.rules.clause}} from the clause modules and their hand-overs, {{f:cell.rules.flush}} at the edges of a clause), {{f:encoder.state_units|,}} state units, {{f:encoder.input_units|,}} input units and {{f:encoder.nnz|,}} nonzeros; the decoder has the {{f:decoder.rules|,}} printing rules, {{f:decoder.state_units|,}} state units, {{f:decoder.output_units|,}} of them the output block, and {{f:decoder.nnz|,}} nonzeros; and a frame of the context is {{f:context.frame_units|,}} units. `np_module` agrees with `parse_np` on all {{f:np.cases|,}} windows of at most {{f:np.max_len}} tokens over {{f:np.alphabet}} token classes. Its two adjective slots refuse an adverb followed by two adjectives, where `parse_np` concatenates. An of-phrase is the module run once more on the role's of-phrase slots, printed before the head with の, and a second of-phrase inside it is refused; a frame whose string came from an inner clause is marked folded and stays outside the encoder's scope. The rule parser refuses all {{f:negatives.count}} negatives and accepts all {{f:negatives.controls_accepted}} controls; the matrix refuses {{f:negatives.matrix_refused}} of the negatives and accepts {{f:negatives.controls_matrix}} of the controls, the rest falling to its extracted clause-level rules. The {{f:printer.rules|,}} printing rules at {{f:printer.positions}} positions print {{f:printer.agree}} of the {{f:printer.parsed}} parsed sentences exactly as `generate()` does and refuse the other {{f:printer.gap}}, whose clauses hold a register the frame has no slot for or, under unless, an ending the rules do not print; on the sweep they print {{f:printer.sweep.match|,}} frames as `generate()` does, refuse {{f:printer.sweep.refused}}, and mismatch on {{f:printer.sweep.mismatches}}. The PP machine, `predicate` and `copula_predicate` are translated by hand in `pp_module.py` and `predicate_module.py` — per position an ordered decision whose last rule is the refusal — and spliced into the encoder at every activation of those methods, so no rule extracted from traces is left for them; what is still extracted is the start of the sentence, the question paths, the verb phrase, its objects and the end. A that-clause after a verb is read into a frame of its own and printed as the rules print it; one after a copula (I am sure that he will come) opens inside the copula module, which refuses it. Hypothesis: the gaps that remain are a that-clause after a copula, a subordinate clause whose conjunction has a second reading, the inner clauses the rule parser still folds into strings and the paths still extracted, and translating those paths by hand would leave the encoder no induced guard.
